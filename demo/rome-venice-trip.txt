### Gianicolo Hill — sunset (optional)
- lat: 41.8916
- lng: 12.4656
- category: view
- duration: 45
- image: https://www.romeloft.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/rome-panorama.jpg
- description: Rome’s best sunset viewpoint, looking out over the entire historic center from a hill inhabited since Etruscan times.
- detail: Not one of Rome’s official “seven hills” (it sits across the Tiber, outside the ancient city walls), the Janiculum was still strategically vital — Garibaldi’s forces defended it against French troops here in 1849 during the fight for a short-lived Roman Republic. A cannon still fires from the hill at noon every day, a 19th-century tradition so Romans could set their clocks.

### Wander Trastevere
- lat: 41.8899
- lng: 12.4694
- category: landmark
- duration: 150
- image: https://www.neverendingvoyage.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/trastevere-rome-10.jpg
- description: Rome’s most atmospheric neighborhood — cobbled lanes and ivy-covered facades that give the city its postcard evening light.
- detail: The name comes from Latin trans Tiberim, ‘across the Tiber’ — for centuries this was Rome’s working-class, immigrant neighborhood, kept apart from the grand imperial city across the river. Its tangle of medieval streets survived largely untouched by later redevelopment, which is why it still feels like the Rome of centuries ago — a look prized by countless film crews shooting “classic Rome” scenes here.

### Walk around Tiber Island
- lat: 41.8906
- lng: 12.4778
- category: landmark
- duration: 40
- image: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Ponte_Cestio%2C_Isola_Tiberina%2C_Roma.jpg?width=1200
- description: A short loop around the little boat-shaped island in the Tiber, two bridges and five minutes from Trastevere — and the Assassins’ hideout in Assassin’s Creed: Brotherhood.
- detail: The ancient Romans faced the island in travertine so it would look like a ship, with an obelisk for a mast; part of the carved “prow” still juts out at the downstream end. A temple to Aesculapius, god of medicine, was founded here in 291 BC after a plague, and the island has never stopped being a place of healing — the Fatebenefratelli hospital has stood on it since 1584, and during the German occupation of Rome its doctors invented a fake, horrifying-sounding illness called “Syndrome K” to hide Jewish refugees in a quarantine ward the Nazis would not enter. Ponte Fabricio, the bridge to the Ghetto side, has carried traffic continuously since 62 BC.

## Day 2: Ancient Rome
- start: 08:30
- hotel: The Tribune, JdV by Hyatt

### Colosseum
- lat: 41.8902
- lng: 12.4922
- category: landmark
- duration: 105
- image: https://cdn-imgix.headout.com/microbrands-content-image/image/e4e2b84ff4e7f818201b1143c7da369e-Colosseum.jpg?auto=compress%2Cformat&q=90&crop=faces&fit=crop
- description: The largest amphitheater the Roman Empire ever built — and the biggest Assassin’s Creed: Brotherhood set piece. Rome’s most crowded sight; pre-book tickets.
- detail: Completed in 80 AD under Emperor Titus, it held an estimated 50,000–80,000 spectators and used a retractable awning system, operated by sailors, to shade the crowd. Its underground chambers (the hypogeum) held animals and gladiators, raised to the arena floor by a network of pulleys and lifts. It's the emotional center of Ridley Scott's Gladiator (1998) — though the film's interior scenes were actually shot on a built set in Malta and the UK, not here.
- tags: ⚔ Assassin's Creed

### Roman Forum + Palatine Hill
- lat: 41.8925
- lng: 12.4853
- category: landmark
- duration: 135
- image: https://amymorgan.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Roman-Forum-and-Palatine-Hill-14.jpg
- description: The political and religious heart of ancient Rome, where the Senate met and emperors lived. One combined ticket covers both.
- detail: This was the civic heart of Rome for over a thousand years — the site of triumphal processions, elections, criminal trials, and public oratory, including some of Cicero's most famous speeches. The Palatine Hill above it is, by tradition, where Romulus and Remus were found and raised, and where emperors later built their palaces — the word “palace” itself comes from “Palatine.”

### Capitoline Museums
- lat: 41.8931

## Day 3: Castel Sant’Angelo + Vatican
- start: 09:00
- hotel: The Tribune, JdV by Hyatt

### Castel Sant’Angelo
- lat: 41.9031
- lng: 12.4663
- category: landmark
- duration: 90
- image: https://italian-traditions.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/castelsantangelo-it.jpg
- description: Hadrian’s mausoleum turned papal fortress — the climactic set piece of Angels & Demons. Closed Mondays.
- detail: Built by Hadrian as his own mausoleum around 139 AD, it was later converted into a papal fortress, connected to the Vatican by a secret elevated passage called the Passetto di Borgo — used at least once for a real papal escape during the 1527 Sack of Rome. Puccini's opera Tosca ends with its heroine leaping from these ramparts, a genuine fixture of Roman opera rather than a modern film reference.
- tags: 📖 Angels & Demons

### Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel
- lat: 41.9065
- lng: 12.4536
- category: museum
- duration: 200
- image: https://media-cdn.tripadvisor.com/media/attractions-splice-spp-674x446/0f/ba/c1/d8.jpg
- description: One of the world’s greatest art collections, ending in Michelangelo’s ceiling. Book skip-the-line tickets weeks ahead. Closed Sundays.
- detail: Housing roughly 70,000 works across a collection so vast that seeing every piece for just one minute each would take over four years. The Sistine Chapel ceiling took Michelangelo about four years to paint, mostly working alone on scaffolding of his own design — the popular image of him painting flat on his back is only partly accurate.

### St. Peter’s Basilica + Square
- lat: 41.9022
- lng: 12.4539
- category: church
- duration: 75
- image: https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTfTnQJzX8IBKA2kgBwQ8bnxw5tJpxdCZHO9hySgIAqSfTGcq24mK8r93Lh&s=10

### Doria Pamphilj Gallery
- lat: 41.8975
- lng: 12.4816
- category: museum
- duration: 75
- image: https://cdn.getyourguide.com/image/format=auto,fit=crop,gravity=auto,quality=60,width=375,height=375,dpr=2/tour_img/a464c6cf22623721d207afc2cab6e634872c82ef26edc46c8b899121651b8c3e.jpg

### Pantheon
- lat: 41.8986
- lng: 12.4769
- category: landmark
- duration: 45
- image: https://ik.imagekit.io/travalot/development/resources/attachments/2026/3/28/02b722e0-42e0-11f1-bfe2-8bcedebd6fff.jpg?tr=w-1600,h-1067,c-at_max:f-webp:q-85
- description: Ancient Rome’s best-preserved building, capped by the largest unreinforced concrete dome ever built. A red-herring "first altar" in Angels & Demons.
- detail: Its dome is still the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world, nearly 1,900 years after it was built. The 8-meter oculus at the top is the building's only light source and has never been covered — rain falls straight through onto a slightly sloped, drained floor. Continuous use since antiquity (first as a temple, then as a church since the 7th century) is largely why it survived intact while other ancient buildings were stripped for materials.

- detail: Built directly over the ruins of the 1st-century Stadium of Domitian, which explains the piazza's long, oval shape — you're standing in the footprint of an ancient running track. Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers personifies the Nile, Ganges, Danube, and Río de la Plata, representing four continents known to Europeans at the time.

### Galleria Borghese
- lat: 41.9142
- lng: 12.4923
- category: museum
- duration: 120
- image: https://romesite.com/images/galleria_borghese.webp
- description: A compact, dazzling Bernini-and-Caravaggio collection inside a cardinal’s garden villa. Mandatory timed entry — book as early as possible. Closed Mondays.

### Gondola ride
- lat: 45.4318
- lng: 12.333
- category: boat
- duration: 30
- image: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Venice_Gondola_Grand_Canal.jpg?width=1200
- description: A classic 30-minute gondola ride through Dorsoduro’s quieter back canals, timed for golden hour. Priced per gondola (up to 5 people), not per person — the standard daytime rate applies until roughly 7pm, after which an evening surcharge kicks in. - detail: Gondolas have been the working transport of Venice since at least the 11th century, and were once so competitively ornate that a 1562 sumptuary law forced them all to be painted black — which is why they still are. Only a few hundred remain today, down from thousands at the Republic’s peak.

- lng: 12.4826
- category: landmark
- duration: 15

- description: Rome’s city hall, on the Capitoline Hill, at the far end of the same piazza as the Capitoline Museums — seen from the piazza rather than visited inside.
- detail: Built over the ancient Tabularium, Rome’s 1st-century-BC public record archive, the palace has served as the seat of Rome’s civic government since medieval times. ### Reservations

**Book weeks ahead — these sell out**
- Galleria Borghese (D5) — mandatory timed entry, no walk-ins ever. Book first, as early as you can; it dictates when Day 5 can start.
- Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel (D3) — skip-the-line, 2–4 weeks ahead. Closed Sundays.
- Colosseum (D2) — timed entry, 2–3 weeks ahead. Covers the Forum/Palatine on one ticket.
- Doge's Palace (D7) — pre-book to skip a long line.
- Rome → Venice train (D6) — Frecciarossa/Italo, 1–2 weeks ahead. Not a sellout risk, but fares climb sharply close to the date.

**Book 3–7 days ahead — restaurants on the plan**
- Antiche Carampane (D8 dinner) — reservation required, deliberately hidden with no sign outside.
- Armando al Pantheon (D4 dinner) — small, beloved, strongly recommended.
- Da Enzo al 29 (D1 dinner) — tiny room, books out fast.
- Trattoria al Gatto Nero (D10 lunch, Burano) — recommended, and you'll be on a fixed vaporetto schedule.
- La Taverna dei Fori Imperiali (D2 lunch) — recommended at peak lunch.
- Hostaria Al Gladiatore (D2 dinner) — recommended on weekends.

**Worth checking before you go**
- Giardini della Biennale (D8) — a ticketed Biennale venue while the exhibition runs (through Nov 22, 2026), not a free public park during that window.
- Gondola ride (D8) — no booking needed; arrive before ~7pm for the standard daytime rate.
- Venice access fee — applies to day-trippers on certain dates; overnight guests staying in Venice are exempt, but worth confirming your dates.
- Capuchin Crypt (D5) and Doria Pamphilj (D4) — timed entry on some dates; check rather than assume walk-in.
